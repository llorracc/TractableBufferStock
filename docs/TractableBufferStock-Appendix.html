Solve the problem for a finite horizon, at the end of which the consumer spends everything, and let the horizon lengthen. Because utility is negative when , each period added to the horizon lowers value. But at every horizon the employed consumer’s value is at least that of an unemployed consumer with the same resources, since the employed consumer could always spend what the unemployed one would. And as the horizon lengthens, the unemployed consumer’s value falls toward , its value with no end to the horizon, which the RIC keeps finite. So the employed consumer’s value functions, falling and bounded below, converge; since they are concave, so do their slopes (the limit is differentiable, since it solves the Bellman equation with spending strictly between zero and resources), and with them, through the envelope condition, the consumption functions.
